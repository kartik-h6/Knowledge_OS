## The question I work on

Everything I build, write, and study circles one question:

> How can healthcare knowledge be communicated, organised, and translated into understanding
> that helps people make better decisions — and ultimately improves health outcomes?

Healthcare rarely suffers from a lack of information. It suffers from the gap between
information and understanding.

## The gap, stated as two failures

1. **The Capability Gap** — health systems produce enormous volumes of evidence-based
   material, which arrives as information overload rather than understanding.
2. **The Comprehension Gap** — at the point of care, patients need immediate, simple, and
   culturally synchronised guidance to stay adherent and manage chronic conditions.

**Working hypothesis:** technology-assisted, vernacular, visual-first communication can
measurably improve adherence and reduce avoidable re-hospitalisation.

## Research

- **Telepharmacy — Comparative Literature Review (2025).** *"Patient Satisfaction and Counseling
  Effectiveness in Telepharmacy: A Comparative Literature Review of Remote vs. Face-to-Face"* —
  RGUHS final-year dissertation, carried out individually from literature search to presentation.
  Satisfaction was high in both models (4.2 remote vs 4.5 face-to-face), but trust and emotional
  reassurance leaned toward in-person care — which reframed the question as *which mode suits a
  given patient at a given point in the care journey*, rather than which model wins.
- **Medical writing sample — AI Patient Education Narrative Review**, written during the medical
