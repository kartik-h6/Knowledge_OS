# LinkedIn carousel — Healthcare Knowledge Essays

The visual system already exists: twenty posters, one per essay, archived with the series. This skill keeps new ones inside it rather than inventing a new look.

## When to use it, and when not to

Use a carousel when the idea has **sequence**: a framework with steps, a season with parts, a before and after, a list that only makes sense in order.

Do not use it for a single claim. One claim belongs in a text post. Splitting one claim across eight slides is the most common way a carousel fails.

## The visual system (match the archive)

- Ground: deep navy, or white with navy type for lighter essays.
- Accent: the brand gradient, cyan to blue. One accent per slide, never two.
- Mark: the hexagon and cube symbol, small, in the header or footer. Never stretched, never recoloured outside the gradient.
- Number: the essay number in the top corner, permanent and matching `04_ESSAY_INDEX.md`.
- Headline: the essay's question, in caps or sentence case, large enough to read at thumbnail size.
- One mental model per carousel: a diagram, a comparison, a sequence. If the idea needs two models, it is two carousels.
- One healthcare example, concrete, usually a drug interaction or a counselling moment.
- Closing slide: the key takeaway in one or two lines, in the essay's own words where possible.

## Slide architecture

1. **Cover** — the question. Nothing else. It has to work at 25% zoom.
2. **The premise** — what is commonly assumed, in two lines.
3. **The problem** — why that assumption is incomplete.
4. **The model** — the framework or comparison, one item per row.
5. **The example** — the healthcare case, stated plainly.
6. **The implication** — what a reader should do differently.
7. **The takeaway** — one line, plus the essay number and the series mark.

Seven slides is the default. Fewer if the idea is thin; never more to look thorough.

## Copy rules

- One idea per slide. If a slide needs a second idea, the carousel is wrong.
- Forty words per slide maximum, excluding the model rows.
- No emoji. No exclamation marks.
- No "swipe to see", no "follow for more", no engagement bait.
- No QR code unless it points to a real, specific resource. The series rule stands.
- Do not put the author's face on a carousel unless the essay is personal.

## Export

- LinkedIn document posts: 1080 × 1350 (4:5), PDF, text as vector where possible so it stays sharp.
- Archive copies: 800 × 1200 (2:3) to match the existing twenty posters.
- Keep the source file. The gallery in the collection is built from these.

## Before delivery

Read the cover at 25% zoom. If the question is not legible, the cover is wrong, not the reader.

Check the number and title against `04_ESSAY_INDEX.md`. A carousel that contradicts the index becomes a permanent inconsistency the moment it is published.
